Tuesday

From the Garden & the Sea

Roasted salmon, lemon & dill

A rich fillet roasted simply, with lemon and dill.

Salmon's richness comes from the fat that fuels its long migrations — the same omega-3s that make it so prized at the table. Dill and lemon are the classic northern foil: grassy, bright and sharp enough to lift the oiliness rather than wrestle with it.

Shaved squash ribbons & roasted radishes

Raw summer squash in a bright, herby dressing, and radishes turned sweet and mellow in brown butter and honey.

Summer squash is picked deliberately immature, which is why the whole thing — skin, seeds and all — stays tender; shaved raw into ribbons it keeps its crisp, grassy snap. The radishes travel the opposite road: roasting tames their peppery bite — a mustard-family kick that evolved to fend off pests — into something gentle and nearly sweet, and brown butter meets it halfway.

A bottle A light Pinot Noir or a rounded, unoaked Chardonnay — both flatter salmon.

Something sweet A lemon tart, or a berry pavlova.

Wednesday

Golden Hour

Golden summer soup

A mellow, brothy pot of squash, carrot and sweet corn, finished with crisp bacon.

This is a soup that lives and dies by its corn: once picked, sweet corn's sugars start turning to starch within a day or two, so the window for that bright, milky sweetness is short and worth chasing. The recipe comes from the Moosewood kitchen — the Ithaca collective whose cookbooks quietly taught a generation of American home cooks that vegetables could carry a whole meal.

A bottle An unoaked white or an off-dry Riesling — a little sweetness echoes the corn.

Something sweet Sweet-corn ice cream, or a simple buttery pound cake.

Thursday

A Bright Fish Braise

Striper chraime

Striper braised in a bold, garlicky, gently spicy tomato-and-pepper sauce, brightened with lemon.

Chraime is a North African Jewish dish — Libyan, Tunisian, Moroccan — and its name comes straight from the Arabic for "hot." It's traditionally a Friday-night and holiday plate, the Sephardic counterpart to Ashkenazi gefilte fish, and it's forgiving by design: the sauce was built to carry whatever firm fish a household had, so it takes well to almost any kind.

A bottle A chilled light red (Grenache) or a bold, dry rosé to ride the spice.

Something sweet Orange-blossom semolina cake, or a clean citrus sorbet.

Friday

A Sonoran Supper

Sonoran-style bacon-wrapped dogs & buttermilk slaw

Bacon-wrapped links tucked into soft rolls, piled with beans, tomato and onion, with a crisp, tangy slaw.

The Sonoran dog is border food with a real hometown: Hermosillo, the capital of Sonora, where late-night cart vendors — dogueros — wrapped franks in bacon and buried them in toppings before the style crossed north to become a signature of Tucson. The bacon was the move that turned an imported novelty into something unmistakably regional; the full build, con todo, sits far closer to a taco cart's idea of a hot dog than a ballpark's.

A bottle A cold lager, or a juicy, chillable Beaujolais.

Something sweet Lime paletas, or warm cinnamon churros.

The Weekend Table

Cook's-Choice Supper

Garden vegetable lasagne

A creamy béchamel lasagne layered with leeks, carrot and broccoli — the easy, comforting close to the week.

This is lasagne in the northern Italian manner — bound with béchamel, one of the French "mother sauces," rather than the tomato-and-ricotta most Americans picture. The shape itself is among the oldest pasta on record, its name traced back through Latin to a Greek word for a flat cooking pot; the ridged sheets are essentially unchanged after centuries.

A bottle A medium Italian red — Chianti or a bright Barbera.

Something sweet Tiramisu, or a scoop of lemon gelato.
