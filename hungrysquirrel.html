    About the Dishes

    Where they come from & why they're good for you
    The Hungry Squirrel
    Field notes · page 2

    This week leans the way a Mediterranean-style diet does — vegetables and fruit up front, lean proteins in support (game, fish, a little poultry), olive oil over butter, and one gloriously indulgent cookout night to keep it human. Here's the story and the upside behind each plate.

    Complete Mediterranean · p. 239
    Ratatouille

    Flavor Eggplant, summer squash, sweet peppers and tomatoes stewed slowly in olive oil until everything melds into a soft, glossy Provençal jumble.

    Roots A Niçoise peasant dish of high-summer surplus — its name comes from the Occitan/French touiller, “to stir.” The trick the good recipes insist on is cooking the vegetables separately before combining, so each keeps its character instead of collapsing into mush.

    In the plan The answer to Week 11’s eggplant-and-pepper heyday — one pot that uses four of the share’s stars. Spoon over roasted purple potatoes or crusty bread; better the next day.

    Six Seasons · p. 244
    Roasted pepper panzanella

    Flavor Blistered, peeled sweet peppers and torn croutons soaked in their juices, oil and vinegar, with red onion, oregano, mint and torn mozzarella.

    Roots Panzanella is Tuscan thrift — a way to make stale bread the point of a salad rather than the waste. The bread wants to sit long enough to drink up the dressing but not so long it turns to mush.

    In the plan Made meatless (salami left out) for the vegetarian kale night; roasts up the CSA sweet peppers, with croutons torn from your house-baked ciabatta; mozzarella and mint already on hand.

    Turkuaz Kitchen · p. 161
    Zucchini & feta biscuits

    Flavor Flaky buttermilk biscuits shot through with grated squash, sharp cheddar, feta and dill — savory, tender, herby.

    Roots A modern mash-up: the American buttermilk biscuit meets the feta-and-herb instinct of Turkish baking.

    Good for you A painless way to slip more summer squash into a meal — grated in, it all but disappears into the crumb.

    In the plan A small bake beside Friday's lentil soup; squeeze the grated squash dry so they don't turn heavy.

    Complete Mediterranean · p. 52
    French lentil soup

    Flavor Earthy lentils built on bacon, onion, carrot and thyme, with tomato and a lift of wine and vinegar at the end.

    Roots Lentils are among the oldest cultivated foods on earth, farmed in the Near East for something like ten thousand years — cheap, storable protein that fed people long before refrigeration. Puy-style green lentils hold their shape instead of dissolving, which is why French cooking treats them as a dish rather than a thickener.

    Good for you High in plant protein and fiber, and the splash of acid at the finish isn't just flavor — vitamin C helps you absorb the iron.

    In the plan A pantry night: nearly every ingredient was already in the house.

    Six Seasons · p. 239
    Rigatoni & eggplant alla Norma

    Flavor Salted, browned eggplant and crumbled sausage tossed with rigatoni, cherry tomatoes, oregano and chile, finished with Pecorino and shaved ricotta salata.

    Roots A Sicilian classic said to be named for Bellini's opera Norma — the story goes a diner called a plate of it a masterpiece worthy of the opera. Salting the eggplant first draws out water and bitterness so it browns instead of steams.

    In the plan The eggplant's real home now — it also leans on the sausage and cherry tomatoes already on the list.

    Complete Mediterranean · p. 211
    Broiled eggplant with basil

    Flavor Eggplant blistered under high heat until creamy, finished with raw basil.

    Roots Eggplant travelled from South Asia through the Arab world into the Mediterranean, where it was long treated with suspicion as a nightshade before becoming indispensable.

    Good for you Two ingredients, almost no fat, and it handles a glut — which is the point in a bumper-crop week.

    Moosewood Daily Special
    Roasted green tomato & feta salad

    Flavor Roasting mellows a green tomato's hard acidity into something tangy and almost savory; feta, olives and mint push it Greek.

    Roots Green tomatoes are simply unripe fruit — historically a way to use what frost or a short season would otherwise waste.

    In the plan Straight from the share, in the week the farm has them and red ones are still a maybe.

    Moosewood Daily Special
    Golden summer soup

    Flavor Mellow and brothy, gently sweet from squash, carrot and corn with a little melting cheese.

    Roots A Moosewood standard — the Ithaca vegetarian institution that shaped American meatless cooking from the 1970s on.

    Good for you Carrots bring beta-carotene (vitamin A); a light pot that the crisped bacon or a can of beans turns into a fuller meal.

    In the plan Day 5, timed to use the corn before its sugars turn starchy — sweet corn is best within a day or two of picking.

    Moosewood Low-Fat · p. 216
    Vegetable lasagne béchamel

    Flavor Creamy white-sauce layers with leeks, carrots and broccoli — comforting and mild.

    Roots Lasagne is among the oldest pasta shapes; the béchamel-layered northern-Italian style (rather than tomato-and-ricotta) is the classic Emilia-Romagna approach.

    Good for you A lighter Moosewood version; leeks (an allium) add prebiotic fiber, and it's a hearty meat-free anchor.

    In the plan Cooked last (Days 8–10) because its leeks keep longest — and it freezes for future weeks. Béchamel is one of the French "mother sauces."

    Eating Out Loud · p. 194
    Braised striper (Chraime)

    Flavor Fish braised in a bold, garlicky, spicy tomato-and-pepper sauce, brightened with lemon.

    Roots A North African Jewish dish — especially Libyan, Moroccan and Tunisian — whose name comes from the Arabic for "hot." Traditionally a Friday-night Shabbat starter (also Rosh Hashanah and Passover), often called the Sephardic counterpart to Ashkenazi gefilte fish. The fish is meant to be whatever's freshest — so your striper is right in the spirit.

    Good for you Lean, high-protein white fish; the tomato-pepper sauce piles on vitamin C and lycopene.

    In the plan Day 6 — a light, fast midweek protein that reuses the cherry tomatoes and tahini already on hand.
